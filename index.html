UPDATES INSTALLED, POSTCULTURE ARE BACK!

Despite an increasingly hostile climate facing grassroots music across the UK*, we’re super-excited to be back with a brand new studio album (our first since 2015) and sounding better than ever!

Updates Install came together over a three year period following Avadance (Basement Tapes) – a compilation of favourite jams – which can now be heard as a companion piece to many tracks on this new album. Are written pieces better than their original improvisations? Spoiler alert: yes!

Lots of people have helped make this happen. Special thanks to Anna for always being available, Sam for his incredible writing, Rosie for her "210%" blessing, Reuben for his patience, and Ruby for the last minute collaboration of our dreams!


UPDATES RECALL


Matt on ‘Catch Me If You Can’

Any memories? No not really cos it’s from a long-long-time ago innit? It just feels like it’s always been how it is now; even though there must’ve been some sort of writing process involved... Surely?! Errm, sorry, bare with, I’m at a wedding… me kids are running riot...

[Two days later] I can remember wanting to put an odd time signature into a dance tune to see if it’d work, because it’s not something you really hear; maybe for a reason? I can remember faffing about with a tapped bassline and an idea developing from there. We tried a section change that gave a bit of let-up from the tapping part, but it just didn’t work rhythmically. That tapping, for me, felt like it needed to be relentless and persist throughout; so our distinction between section changes became more chordal.

I can remember thinking, “OMG what have I let me sen in for with this bass part!?” Think I must’ve been listening to a lot of Primus? It is quite reminiscent of DMV. Anyway, really proud of how it turned out, but by the end, hated it to play. With practise came stamina! Adding some chorus to the tone was icing ont cake and now I love the tune! Well done us... especially me.

○ Original improv January 26th 2018 ○

Tom on ‘Aftersun’

What a bassline!

Perhaps the jam that led to this entire album? An incredibly rare case of an improv working so well, simply relearn and you’ve got a finished track! Not quite that easy perhaps but surprising how close this version is from its progenitor.

Pretty sure all the synths are Oberheim SEM, with the definite exception of some Absynth 5 pads played via guitar MIDI pickup. We're pretty faithful to the 2022 improv but a proper intro and outro had to be written. See if you can pick out the 17/16 guitar riff!

Also a rare example of a working title that stuck. Initial jam came two days after I’d seen Charlotte Wells’ debut for the second time in a week. Was obviously on my mind as I mixed down our jams from the day, and remains on my mind now.

○ Original improv November 22nd 2022, as heard on Avadance (Basement Tapes) ○


Jonny on ‘Still We Rise’

I remember the jam where Matt came up with the “womping” bassline which we then wrote the track around; then re-wrote the track around; then re-re-wrote track around; then re-re-re-wrote…

There were a lot of versions of this track!

We even recorded a version during the Pineapple sessions in 2019 - but were never totally happy with it. Tom instigated the final re-write in 2023 and we finally had a track we felt good enough to record and release as an instrumental…

BUT THEN, at the very last minute, Ruby Wood (of Submotion Orchestra fame - one of the biggest influences on Postculture) made herself available for collabs, and that was an offer we could not miss. Cue another slight re-write and here you have it: Still We Rise!

○ Original improv March 25th 2017 ○

Matt on ‘Updates Install’

I can remember that Tom had either got some new software or a new synth or summat†. Scrolling through sounds he landed on that arpeggiator and straight away I was like, “that one!” I wanted a track building everything off of that arp: bass, drums, guitars and any additional synths; so that’s what we did! The bassline was written in next to no time around the arpeggiator by picking out allt’ main notes and adding in a few extra root notes for good measure. Incidentally, it ended up being in 6/4 and grooving like a mothaf**ker!

And it was one of the first times we actually thought properly about how to write a tune that worked as an instrumental track; and I think it shows. The structure, the groove, our placement of dropouts, the way it builds throughout, the sample that Tom used and how it’s placed… I think we hit the nail ont head. Really does feel like we’ve upped our game and Updates Install, for me, had set a new precedent for what our music wants to sound like. It was as if POSTCULTURE themselves had had updates installed!

○ Original improv November 24th 2021, as heard on Avadance (Basement Tapes) ○

† Tom clarifies: A Korg Monologue analogue synth. My laptop chose to install updates mid-session, hence switching over to hardware.


Jonny on ‘Lockdown’

My favourite song on the album.

Like Aftersun this came from a pretty complete, structured jam, which we then attempted to learn.

As a drummer, the main purpose during jams is to keep time, which consequently leads to them not containing the most interesting drum parts to play. As a result, “attempting to learn” for me usually just means writing a completely new part. Other than the intro, that was the case with this song.

Me and Matt wrote the “verse” groove together - which I especially like - and the middle section fulfills a long time wish of mine to have an extended section of a song without drums and bass!

○ Original improv October 29th 2020, as heard on Avadance (Basement Tapes) ○

Tom on ‘Dill Buffer (Interlude)’ / Taking Hold

Initially a guitar melody and chord sequence which went through a couple of iterations before Jonny’s drum groove solidified the Taking Hold you hear today. We shared an early instrumental online looking for collaborators, Leo Transit answered the call. That vocal sampled in the Dill Interlude is from a live runthrough, he’s singing a first draft vocal. By the time we hit the studio years later, Leo Transit had relocated to South Korea, replacing his microphone for a wife and son. Got permission to finish his melody, rewriting lyrics alongside Anna. She sounds better every year and we remain ever grateful she’s been a part of our journey this whole time. Side note: can’t remember if the arp is a Juno 6 or SH-101? I’d quite like to know!

Matt on ‘Preage / Triage’

Anything we do musically, we gotta add a bit o’ Yorkshire into it; our inspiration! I don’t think we could have a release that didn’t include summat Yorkshire related. And, for me, grainy old audio samples set to a backdrop of ambience do sound mint.

I was listening to a lot of Tool, particularly their Lateralus album. Was (and still am) really into two tracks that start with the bass playing harmonics. Proper earthy, ambient, tribal sounding, and one track leads seamlessly into the next. Really liked that idea and how they achieved it... could that work in our context?

I think so! But, for us though, wanted to progress towards a big, dancey groove; which is where the idea of Tom’s chimes taking over from my bass harmonics, came in. That way, I could switch to a more ballsy, relentless bass groove throughout, throwing in the odd chord change to break it up. A really simple but disciplined part that’d go hand in hand with Jonny’s brutal, hi-hat happy drum groove, whilst still giving Tom room to play about with his guitar, arp and synth parts, which become the main things giving the tune its structure.

Again, I think we smashed what we set out to achieve with these two tracks. I can’t listen to one without t’other. Triage feels like a hark back to our first eight minute dance epic, A New Day, but with a new approach to writing instrumental tunes for an instrumental band. Not to mention ten more years of trial and error on our side!

○ Original improv November 22nd 2022, as heard on Avadance (Basement Tapes) ○

Jonny on ‘Gold Dust (210% Blessed Mix)’

Wait, Postculture mull over ideas for a long time?!?

Gold Dust came together as an instrumental fairly quickly, and we even gigged it a bit in 2018/19. However we always knew this wasn't going to be an entirely instrumental track; the problem was we had no idea what to put on it! We tried a few singers over the years, but never really had anything which felt totally right.

I wanted to write the simplest drum part possible for this song; real page one of the “drumming for beginners” book stuff. I like the song all the more for that.

Jump forward to 2026 and we remembered that a few years ago Rosie Blofeld had sent us one of the tracks off her last EP to remix (which is a story in its own right). Putting her vocal track over the instrumental worked perfectly! It was the same tempo and key - almost like it was meant to be. Thankfully Rosie was kind enough to bless our remix “210%”!

○ Original improv July 4th 2018 ○

The Lads by Lauren Russell


No AI was used in the creation of this album.

Not a disclaimer we’ve ever needed to include on any of our releases thus far but worth clarifying a point your ears are likely capable of confirming.

As the term “AI” remains incredibly vague, this feels an apt place to discuss the steps that actually went into the creation of Updates Install. After all, we didn’t arrive at a recording studio, mic up the band, hit the big red button, then release the results. Various degrees of manipulation go into virtually all digital music production. If you’ve not been producing music this century, here's some insight…

Every track Postculture have released, started from improvisation. Primarily three of us in a basement together with a room mic set to record. Sometimes alongside a singer; perhaps only two of us present; very occasionally from an idea brought to a session by one person. Our 2023 compilation Avadance (Basement Tapes) presents several of these, in edited down forms. Condensing long and meandering jams to their “best bits” has always been regular practice. Most of these improvs remain moments captured in time, yet sometimes an idea stands the test of time.

Initiated in 2023 by a desire to turn Avadance into tracks we could play live, it soon became apparent that a new Postculture studio album was materialising. Urged forward by the imminent arrival of Matt's daughter, by April 2024 a selection of tracks were (for the most part) honed, rehearsed and studio ready.

A weekend at Vibrations Studios, Huddersfield, was used to lay down live drum, bass and guitar takes. Sharing a room, we capture three or four takes per track, bass and guitar amps tracked elsewhere leaving clean drum recordings. Then begins an assembly period where preferred takes are selected. This is macro-editing: picking a favourite overall take, swapping out a section if required. Matching instrument takes kept together wherever possible, accepting several individual fixes may be unavoidable. Multiple guitar takes combined for double-tracking purposes.

A common practice applied to nearly all radio music over the last several decades is automatic beat quantisation: pressing a button and having rhythms within an instrumental track line-up perfectly to a tempo grid. This has never been referred to as an “AI” process, although could arguably be one. Due to the live-band-first method of recording, any quantising we opt for is done manually; a time-consuming process that enables a musical accuracy, opposed to metronomic precision. Nearly the only examples across this entire record are the tightening up of several kick-only sections to lock with arpeggiators. If you want perfectly quantised drums, we wouldn’t advise recording in a large room with 16 microphones. Some arpeggiators were actually reverse-quantised manually, allowing them to sit within the drum pocket of a preferred take.

Assembly complete, a brief return to Vibrations Studios in May 2024 to re-amplify final guitar and bass parts. All synth parts were run through hardware, giving life to what can often be undynamic sounds when combined with a live band. Synth parts themselves, a combination of instruments triggered live during improvs, programmed hardware and/or software, plus MIDI guitar parts rewritten and replayed for the studio. Tripleplay’s wireless guitar pickup (turning guitar notes into MIDI data) has been fundamental to the sound of Postculture. Nearly every synth on our first album was, at some point of the songwriting process, played live via guitar. One thing we did for the first time here was hire a village hall to capture what happens when various synths, guitars and samples are blasted through a PA at high volume.

Another historically controversial recording staple is pitch correction. The only instrument requiring this significantly was the Aftersun main arp. Upon resampling from our original jam, turned out it had been running slightly flat. No pitch correction was required on any of the lead vocals. Regarding toplines: at the time of recording, Taking Hold was the only song with a guide vocal. A Leo Transit written part that Anna Shotter - of Sunsound fame - stepped in to complete with us, recording April 2025 at PCHQ. Ruby Wood - Submotion Orchestra - put out a call for collaborators in February 2026. Within the month she had written and self-recorded her Still We Rise vocal. We swiftly arranged and produced; one final addition to a track that was set to be instrumental. Rosie Blofeld - Felka - had sent a vocal to us looking for a remix, back in 2020. It was a fortuitous day discovering Gold Dust worked perfectly with our album closer, an instrumental we’d been looking to complete for years.

Mixing took place sporadically between August 2024 and May 2026 at Reuben Duckering’s home studio in Oxford. In the case of Preage/Triage he was presented with a modest 50 channels to start work on. All in a day’s work! Virtual meetings allowed the four of us to collaborate, with Tom attending many sessions in person. Always a preferable way to work, although anyone who’s had to work with Tom may contest this. Cicely Balston had already mastered three of the tracks at Abbey Road Studios back in Summer 2025. Completing their work in June 2026 at recently acquired New Ground Audio studios in Soho, London.

Hopefully this was an informative glimpse into a few steps that go towards making a Postculture album in 2026. And it didn’t even touch on the artwork commissioned, photography, merchandise, website redesign, nor promotional content created pre-release…

This dissection may also provide one answer to the question: where did all the bands go?

So WHAT'S ALL THIS REPLAY MALARKEY?

Ay up, fancy seeing you here!

We kicked off 2025 by revisiting our back catalogue. (Nearly) every released song, (nearly) every released skit, relearned then recording for posterity in our HD9 rehearsal room, PCHQ.

Does our work stand-up to the Great American Songbook?  Decide for yourself.  We're using this as an excuse to reflect on our writing over the past decade whilst warming the limbs... in preperation for what, you ask?  All will be revealed!

Our material up until AVADANCE (2023) was written with vocals in mind.  If you've seen us live at any point, chances are you saw a primarily instrumental set.  Sampled vocals would be added to A New Day, Feel The Same and Not Enough but we mostly stuck to karaoke versions.

Therefore these REPLAY versions are the first time an audience outside of the UK can glimpse a live Postculture experience.  Albeit not at gig volume, without the sweat of a live venue, and played by three musicians who aren't too familiar with the material.

Postculture are a West Yorkshire-based indie trio.

Jonny (drums), Matt (bass) and Tom (guitar/synths) have been making music together since late 2013. Dance sensibilities inform the writing. However, metal, progressive rock and trip-hop influences permeate, despite valiant attempts to craft pop music. Comparisons have been drawn with MGMT, New Order and The Blockheads.

From the eponymous debut in 2015, through to latest release Updates Install, Postculture have collaborated with some of Yorkshire’s finest singer-songwriters. The likes of Ruby Wood, Sinead Campbell and Adam Taylor (ADMT) all complimenting tracks covering a broad stylistic range. This diverse sound palette always well-received by a strong local following.

Updates Install, the band’s second full-length studio album, prioritises instrumentals over vocal-led songwriting for the first time. “Audiences have long been used to hearing our setlists performed instrumentally,” says Tom. “Actively writing without vocals in mind has definitely resulted in the most honest Postculture album to date. See what you think!”
